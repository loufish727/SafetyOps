# Workplace safety software research

**Prepared:** July 30, 2026  
**Decision context:** One company, five operating locations  
**Goal:** Replace paper forms, spreadsheets, training binders, certificate trackers, and fragmented safety documentation with one virtual system.

## Executive conclusion

The market divides into three product families:

1. **Frontline digitization platforms** such as SiteDocs and SafetyCulture. These are easiest to adopt and strongest at replacing paper forms, checklists, signatures, and daily field work.
2. **Construction workflow platforms** such as SALUS and HammerTech. These connect workers, subcontractors, qualifications, permits, JHAs, equipment, and jobsite access.
3. **Broader EHS suites** such as EHS Insight, HSI Donesafe, Novara Flex, VelocityEHS, and EcoOnline. These provide deeper compliance, risk, training, chemical, document, and reporting capabilities, but require more implementation and administration.

For one company with five locations, the strongest buying shortlist is:

1. **SafetyCulture** for the best overall balance of inspections, training, documents, actions, mobile usability, and self-service implementation.
2. **SiteDocs** when the primary goal is converting existing paperwork, signatures, toolbox talks, and certifications into an uncomplicated field workflow.
3. **EHS Insight** when the company wants a more formal EHS operating system with document control, incidents, CAPA, training, audits, and location dashboards.
4. **SALUS** when the workforce is construction/trades-heavy and worker readiness, certificates, permits, and offline jobsite work matter most.
5. **Novara Flex** when a deep course library, SDS access, OSHA administration, and configurable approval workflows justify a heavier system.

The prototype should not copy one vendor. Its strongest direction is:

- EHS Insight’s command-center dashboard and quick links
- SiteDocs’ low-friction worker queue, signatures, and location-based forms
- SafetyCulture’s training model, site hierarchy, and unified navigation
- SALUS’ worker-readiness and training-gate concepts
- HSI Donesafe’s configurable workflow and controlled-document model
- HammerTech’s onboarding, qualification, permit, and subcontractor patterns

## Detailed product profiles

### 1. SafetyCulture

SafetyCulture began as a mobile inspection product and has expanded into a broader workplace-operations platform. Its major modules include templates, inspections, schedules, actions, issues, investigations, training, documents, assets, sensors, lone-worker monitoring, announcements, analytics, and contractor management. The platform’s operating model is “capture → act → improve”: workers capture field information, managers assign and close actions, and analytics identify trends.

Training is one of its clearest advantages over SiteDocs. Current vendor materials advertise more than 1,000 editable courses, AI-assisted authoring, PowerPoint/PDF/Word conversion, quizzes, practical assessments, group training, translations, narration, certificates, and role/location assignment. See [SafetyCulture Training](https://safetyculture.com/training/) and its [training overview](https://help.safetyculture.com/003441).

For five locations, SafetyCulture supports an organization/area/site hierarchy, inherited site membership, location-aware assignments, and site time zones. Importantly, document classification and document permission are separate concepts: assigning a site helps filtering but does not automatically grant file access. See [sites and levels](https://help.safetyculture.com/001614), [access management](https://help.safetyculture.com/003103), and [Documents](https://help.safetyculture.com/005240).

Its web interface uses a unified left sidebar and opens many details in side panels, preserving the user’s place in a list. Mobile uses bottom navigation with an additional “More” area. The home experience combines training, issues, in-progress work, an agenda, and urgent personal-safety controls. Its visual system is clean, spacious, purple-accented, and card-based.

Current US pricing shows a free tier, Lite from $5 per seat/month annually, Premium at $24 per full seat/month annually or $29 monthly, and custom Enterprise pricing. Seat types and activity limits matter: inexpensive seats may not support active frontline inspection volume. See [pricing](https://safetyculture.com/pricing/) and [seat types](https://help.safetyculture.com/001741).

**Main differentiator:** the strongest balance of inspections, real training, documents, analytics, and approachable deployment.

**Main risks:** module breadth can feel cluttered; advanced functions are plan-dependent; offline behavior varies by module and normally requires pre-sync or download.

### 2. SiteDocs

SiteDocs is the clearest digital replacement for safety binders. Workers complete custom forms, sign records, attach photos, review resources, manage certificates, and respond to assigned tasks in the mobile app. Administrators build templates, assign workers and locations, monitor submissions, and analyze compliance in an office-facing panel.

Major capabilities include custom forms, repeatable/collapsible sections, photos, annotations, voice entry, signatures with date/time/GPS evidence, offline records, safety manuals, SDS files, incidents, hazards, inspections, equipment, orientations, certifications, corrective actions, scheduled forms, dashboards, API, Zapier, and Dropbox. See [SiteDocs features](https://www.sitedocs.com/features/) and the [product overview](https://www.sitedocs.com/).

Locations are first-class records. Workers, forms, templates, and documents can be assigned by location; administrators can choose between broad worker visibility and assigned-worker visibility. See [location management](https://sitedocs.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015290291-Managing-Locations-101) and [template visibility](https://sitedocs.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360012451632-Form-Template-Visibility-Assigning-A-Form-To-A-Specific-Location).

SiteDocs training is best understood as toolbox talks, orientations, attendance/signatures, recurring assignments, completion records, certification storage, and expiry tracking. It is not presented as a deep course-authoring LMS.

The mobile home resembles a worker to-do list: scheduled tasks, overdue/due/upcoming counts, in-progress forms, signed documents, follow-ups, resources, workers, and chat. The Admin Monitor behaves more like an inbox with grouping and saved views. This separation keeps the frontline simple, though managers may switch between worker and admin contexts.

Pricing is quote-based and tied to company size. SiteDocs advertises unlimited forms and signatures; vendor materials describe typical medium-size implementation around 30 days, with some multi-location launches in under ten days. See [pricing](https://www.sitedocs.com/pricing/) and [FAQ](https://www.sitedocs.com/faq/).

**Main differentiator:** the shortest conceptual path from an existing paper safety program to a digital, signed audit trail.

**Main risks:** training depth is limited compared with a full LMS; separate worker/admin surfaces add context switching; real-device testing should focus on sync, signatures, and certificate handling.

### 3. EHS Insight

EHS Insight is a modular EHS/ESG platform covering incidents, investigations, CAPA, audits, inspections, observations, training, meetings, JSAs, risks, permits, documents, SDS, vendors, skills, onboarding, claims, quality, and sustainability. It is broad enough to serve as the company’s safety system of record without necessarily requiring enterprise scale.

A common workflow begins with a worker report or inspection, routes it to a location manager, creates an investigation and CAPAs, escalates overdue tasks, and feeds site/corporate dashboards. Native mobile/offline support covers inspections, incidents, observations, and JHAs.

The user interface is especially useful as a design benchmark. A left rail exposes My Tasks, Forms, Documents, Lists, Reports, and Administration. The main dashboard combines TRIR/LTIR and other KPIs, a My Tasks queue, CAPA completion/overdue visualization, announcements, and Quick Links for incident, CAPA, audit, observation, and meeting workflows. Mobile retains a recognizable card-based dashboard and exposes Offline Sync.

Document Control includes versions, scheduled review, publishing notifications, employee acknowledgement, and review activity. Training covers assignments, completion, certification expiry, meetings, and external course integrations.

Official pricing states that smaller/midsize implementations start around $5,000 annually, varying by contacts, modules, and support. Standard onboarding is described as 30–60 days; enterprise rollouts average three to nine months. See [pricing](https://www.ehsinsight.com/solutions/platform/pricing), [implementation](https://www.ehsinsight.com/solutions/platform/ehs-implementation-process), and [platform](https://www.ehsinsight.com/solutions/platform).

**Main differentiator:** a strong mid-market balance between frontline usability and formal EHS administration.

**Main risks:** configuration and reporting still require a knowledgeable owner; workflow structures may be less freely customizable than “configurable” implies.

### 4. SALUS

SALUS is organized around construction sites, workers, subcontractors, forms, certificates, equipment, incidents, and corrective actions. Field workflows include JHAs/JSAs, pre-task plans, daily logs, permits, inspections, equipment checks, photos/video, voice entry, and verified signatures.

Its training model is distinctive. Short multilingual modules can appear inside the exact workflow that creates the risk. A worker may be required to complete and understand a refresher before signing a high-risk form, opening a permit, operating equipment, or entering a site. Completions, language, scores, signatures, and workflow context land on the worker’s profile. See [SALUS Training](https://www.salussafety.io/us/platform/training).

Each site has its own dashboard, orientation, access, documents, forms, settings, and QR code, while company dashboards aggregate multiple sites. The mobile interface organizes Forms, Inbox, Timers, Sites, and My Toolkit; forms are separated into New, Drafts, Submitted, and Shared.

SALUS emphasizes offline-first work for forms, inspections, incidents, SDS, certificates, training, and evidence, though messaging still requires connectivity. See [offline capabilities](https://www.salussafety.io/us/platform/offline).

**Main differentiator:** worker readiness and training are connected directly to authorization to perform work.

**Main risks:** construction terminology may feel unnatural for permanent non-construction facilities; pricing is quote-based; reporting and bulk administration require live validation.

### 5. Novara Flex, formerly KPA Flex

Novara Flex is a traditional general EHS suite. It covers incidents, corrective actions, inspections, audits, training, SDS, assets, QR codes, compliance calendars, contractors, OSHA recordkeeping, ESG, forms, workflows, dashboards, and integrations.

Its strongest areas are regulatory administration and content. Vendor materials advertise more than 1,000 courses and a 70-million-record SDS database. Resource Management supports office files, video, tags, PDF text search, permissions, offline availability, and revision history. See [EHS software](https://novara.com/ehs-software/), [training](https://novara.com/safety-compliance-training/), and [resource management](https://novara.com/ehs-software/resource-management/).

The interface centers on an administrator control panel, widget dashboards, configurable forms, deep permissions, batch administration, scheduled reports, and mobile access. A June 2026 Form Agent release advertises conversion of PDFs/scans into forms and workflows.

Pricing is quote-based. A preconfigured construction blueprint advertises 26 forms, 76 resources, 26 training modules, five roles, and implementation under eight weeks. See the [construction blueprint](https://novara.com/ehs-software/flex-blueprint-construction/).

**Main differentiator:** deep combination of course content, SDS, OSHA support, configurable forms, permissions, and compliance reporting.

**Main risks:** greater setup and permission complexity; opaque package boundaries; the 2026 company/product transition should be addressed during procurement.

### 6. HSI Donesafe

Donesafe is built around connected registers and a no-code configuration layer. Its modules span incidents, hazards, injuries, audits, inspections, risk, CAPA, obligations, contractors, chemicals, SDS, certifications, learning, inductions, documents, quality, change, assets, fleet, and ESG.

The interface presents role-specific dashboards, global search, a prominent Add New action, registers/lists, record pages, smart forms, and drag-and-drop workflow/dashboard administration. The worker’s experience can be extremely simple while administrators configure conditional logic, approvals, alerts, escalations, and cross-register relationships.

Document control is particularly strong: version history, approvals, acknowledgement, gated access, e-signatures, notifications, and audit trails. See [how it works](https://www.donesafe.com/ehs-platform/how-it-works/), [learning management](https://www.donesafe.com/module/learning-management/), and [document management](https://donesafe.com/uk/module/document-management/).

**Main differentiator:** the most flexible no-code process model in the comparison.

**Main risks:** configuration debt, permission complexity, reporting administration, and the need for a committed internal platform owner.

### 7. HammerTech

HammerTech follows the construction lifecycle: configure projects and subcontractors, register workers, collect qualifications, complete multi-level orientations, submit JHAs/SDS/permits/equipment, run inspections, resolve findings, and roll up project/company performance.

Its worker onboarding is a standout. Workers can receive an SMS/link, verify their phone, complete sequential orientation sections and quizzes, upload qualifications, sign, and receive a worker ID without becoming normal paid software users. The desktop UI is project-centric, with configurable tiles, a project switcher, registers, and a permit board. The mobile inspection experience supports progress, photos, voice-to-text, observations, and attendees.

Pricing is based on construction volume, active projects, complexity, onboarding, and support—not normal per-seat licensing. See [platform](https://www.hammertech.com/en-us/product/platform), [pricing](https://www.hammertech.com/en-us/pricing), and [orientations](https://www.hammertech.com/en-us/platform/orientations-worker-info).

**Main differentiator:** the best subcontractor onboarding, orientation, qualification, and permit/JHA relationships.

**Main risks:** construction-specific concepts and narrower offline completion behavior; not ideal for five simple permanent facilities.

### 8. EcoOnline

EcoOnline combines EHS, chemicals/SDS, training, ESG, lone-worker, and emergency/crisis tools. Its EHS suite covers incidents, inspections, audits, risks/JHAs, CAPA, toolbox talks, permits, contractors, training, and documents.

Public interfaces show a module-launcher dashboard and a worker task dashboard focused on Overdue, Due Today, Assigned to Me, and calendar work. QR codes support rapid hazard and near-miss reporting. Its LMS supports internal/external workers, custom and prebuilt content, learning paths, SCORM/xAPI, video, documents, quizzes, instructor-led training, and certification reminders.

See the [multi-site EHS platform](https://www.ecoonline.com/en-us/ehs-software/), [LMS](https://www.ecoonline.com/safety-learning-management-system/training-content-core/), and [training register](https://www.ecoonline.com/en-us/safety-learning-management-system/training-safety-register/).

**Main differentiator:** strongest combined chemical/SDS, EHS, and training proposition.

**Main risks:** suite breadth may feel fragmented; pricing is opaque; reporting/customization and offline module coverage should be tested.

### 9. VelocityEHS

VelocityEHS Accelerate unifies a location tree, actions, roles, and dashboards across safety, control of work, operational risk, health, ergonomics, environmental compliance, and ESG. Safety covers incidents, audits, inspections, observations, compliance, meetings, CAPA, and training.

The interface uses a dark product sidebar, large solution tiles, KPI dashboards, action queues, location rollups, and mobile capture for incidents, hazards, inspections, photos, and voice input. It is the strongest benchmark for industrial risk, ergonomics, chemical safety, and predictive/AI-assisted EHS.

See [Accelerate](https://www.ehs.com/accelerate/), [Safety](https://www.ehs.com/solution/safety/), and [mobile apps](https://www.ehs.com/mobile-apps/).

**Main differentiator:** enterprise industrial depth with one location/action framework.

**Main risks:** likely excessive for five uncomplicated sites; product/module navigation and pricing need careful scoping.

### 10. Procore Quality & Safety

Procore embeds safety records in a larger construction-management environment containing drawings, RFIs, logs, photos, schedules, directories, and financial workflows. Corporate templates flow into projects; inspections create observations and corrective work; incident data stays attached to the project record.

The UI is polished and familiar to construction teams. It combines company/project switching, tool menus, registers, mobile inspections with large response controls, photos/comments, action plans, and strong folder/version/permission document management.

See [Quality & Safety](https://www.procore.com/quality-safety), [pricing](https://www.procore.com/pricing), and [company documents](https://support.procore.com/products/online/user-guide/company-level/documents).

**Main differentiator:** strongest integration of safety evidence with the wider construction project record.

**Main risks:** it is not a complete EHS suite or turnkey safety LMS; best value exists only when Procore is already the project system of record.

## User-interface findings

Across the market, the strongest interface patterns are consistent:

- A persistent company/location context
- A personalized “Today” or “My Tasks” queue
- One dominant quick-report/inspection action
- A module-oriented desktop sidebar and simplified mobile bottom navigation
- Compact KPI cards paired with overdue/exception queues
- Side panels or drawers that preserve list context
- Clear states for draft, due, overdue, failed, under review, and complete
- Direct linkage from a failed response to its corrective action
- Worker profiles that combine training, certificates, location, role, and readiness
- Visible offline/sync state rather than a vague offline checkbox

The largest design weakness in many enterprise systems is that administrators receive flexibility at the cost of frontline simplicity. SafetyOps should deliberately present two experiences over one data model:

1. **Worker mode:** Today, quick report, start assigned form, training, resources, credentials, sync state.
2. **Manager mode:** multi-location dashboard, exceptions, assignments, investigations, actions, content, analytics, and administration.

## Product requirements derived from the research

### Prototype/MVP

- Five-location dashboard and persistent location selector
- Unified Today/My Work queue
- Digital form and inspection templates
- Signed/versioned inspection submissions
- Incident and near-miss quick report
- Investigation and corrective action register
- Training courses, quizzes, assignments, completion records, and toolbox attendance
- Worker directory with role, location, training, and credentials
- Controlled documents with owner, version, effective/review dates, access, and acknowledgement
- Corporate/location dashboards
- Append-only audit events
- Responsive mobile field experience

### Next functional layer

- Conditional form builder and recurring schedules
- Offline drafts and idempotent synchronization queue
- Evidence uploads and resumable transfer
- Practical training verification
- Certification renewal workflows
- QR-based form, equipment, location, and resource access
- Contractor and visitor onboarding
- Equipment inspections and operator authorization
- Notifications and escalation
- PDF/CSV audit exports

## Research limitations

The comparison uses current vendor documentation, help centers, public pricing, public screenshots, and verified review aggregators. It does not replace hands-on access to paid product environments. Before procurement, each finalist should demonstrate the same company-specific workflow on a real phone:

1. Assign training at one location.
2. Complete a field inspection offline.
3. Fail an item and attach evidence.
4. Create and close a corrective action.
5. Publish a revised document and collect acknowledgement.
6. Export the complete audit trail.

Testing one identical workflow across finalists will expose more meaningful differences than a feature checklist.
